--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[1] WS2812B NEOPIXEL RING — 16 LEDs
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Ring pin        Uno pin        Wire colour (typical)
  ─────────────   ───────────    ──────────────────────
  DIN (data in)   D6             White / Green
  +5V             5V             Red
  GND             GND            Black

  • Data flows IN through DIN. If your ring has DOUT, leave it unconnected
    (or chain a second ring from it).
  • Power draw: up to ~60 mA/LED at full white = ~960 mA for 16 LEDs.
    The sketch caps brightness at 90/255, so worst case is ~340 mA. Still
    above what USB alone likes to supply alongside a servo — see POWER.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[2] ACTIVE 5V BUZZER (2-pin, has internal oscillator)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Buzzer pin      Uno pin
  ─────────────   ───────────
  + (long leg)    D8
  − (short leg)   GND

  • "Active" means it beeps when given DC. A 3-pin "passive" buzzer will
    only click — that type needs a PWM tone() signal instead of HIGH/LOW.
  • The sketch drives D8 HIGH for 80 ms on alerts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[3] SG90 SERVO — VALENCE GAUGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Servo wire      Uno pin        Colour (typical)
  ─────────────   ───────────    ─────────────────
  Signal          D9             Orange / Yellow
  +5V             5V             Red
  GND             GND            Brown / Black

  • Needle scale: 0° = ANGRY, 90° = NEUTRAL, 180° = HAPPY.
  • The sketch eases the angle one degree at a time to keep a cheap SG90
    from stalling on large swings.
  • IMPORTANT: the Servo library uses Timer1, which disables analogWrite()
    PWM on pins 9 and 10. Those pins are fine as digital outputs (as here),
    but do not move the servo to a pin you need PWM on.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[4] 16x2 I2C LCD (PCF8574 backpack, address 0x27)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LCD pin         Uno pin
  ─────────────   ───────────
  VCC             5V
  GND             GND
  SDA             A4
  SCL             A5

  • On the Uno, A4/A5 ARE the hardware I2C pins — the SDA/SCL pins near
    AREF on R3 boards are the same bus, wired in parallel. Use either.
  • Address is 0x27 on most backpacks; 0x3F on some. If the LCD lights but
    prints nothing, run an I2C scanner sketch and change LCD_ADDRESS.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
POWER
================================================================================
  Recommended: power the Uno through USB, and give the NeoPixel ring and
  servo their own 5 V supply (shared GND with the Uno).

  Both the ring and the SG90 are current-hungry. An SG90 can pull
  500–700 mA when stalling. On USB power alone, a servo sweep combined
  with a bright ring will brown out the board and reset it mid-session —
  the symptom is the LCD flashing and the serial link dropping.

  A 5 V / 2 A external supply feeding the ring and servo (with grounds
  tied to the Uno's GND) removes that entirely. Never feed 5 V into the
  servo/ring from the Uno's 5V pin while also powering the Uno from USB.

  ┌────────────────┐
  │ 5V 2A PSU      │
  │  + ────────────┼──► NeoPixel +5V, Servo red
  │  − ────────────┼──► NeoPixel GND, Servo brown, AND Uno GND (common ground)
  └────────────────┘

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SIGNAL INTEGRITY NOTES
================================================================================
  • Adafruit_NeoPixel briefly disables interrupts while shifting data out.
    On an SG90 this can show up as faint jitter of the needle. It is
    cosmetic and does not affect accuracy; the servo easing in the sketch
    keeps it imperceptible. If it bothers you, power the servo separately
    — most reported jitter is actually a voltage sag, not timing.
  • Keep the NeoPixel data wire under ~30 cm. Longer runs without a
    300–470 Ω series resistor on DIN can produce corrupted colours.
  • A 1000 µF electrolytic capacitor across the ring's 5 V and GND is
    the Adafruit-recommended protection against inrush current spikes.
